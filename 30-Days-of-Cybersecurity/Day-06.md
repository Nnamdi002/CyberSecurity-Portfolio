## Overview

Today's class introduced the fundamental principles of cybersecurity, focusing on protecting digital information, controlling access to systems, understanding cyber threats and recognising the importance of personal data security.

## 1. Understanding Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computer systems, networks, devices and data from digital attacks, unauthorised access and damage.

I learned that cybersecurity is important for individuals, organisations and governments because cyberattacks can result in financial losses, privacy breaches and disruption of essential services.

## 2. The CIA Triad

One of the key concepts covered was the **CIA Triad**, which represents three fundamental principles of information security.

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confidentiality | Ensuring information is accessible only to authorised individuals |
| Integrity | Maintaining the accuracy and reliability of information |
| Availability | Ensuring systems and information remain accessible when needed |

**My Understanding:** Effective cybersecurity involves protecting information from unauthorised disclosure, preventing improper modifications and ensuring continued access to systems.

## 3. The AAA Framework

We also explored the AAA Framework, which is used to manage access and accountability within computer systems.

- **Authentication:** Verifying a user's identity.
- **Authorization:** Determining what an authenticated user is permitted to access or perform.
- **Accounting:** Recording and monitoring user activities.

**Key Difference:** Authentication confirms who a user is, while authorization determines what the user can do.

## 4. Understanding Cyber Threats

The class introduced important cybersecurity terminology:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Threat | Anything capable of causing harm to a system or information |
| Vulnerability | A weakness that could be exploited |
| Exploit | A method used to take advantage of a vulnerability |
| Target | The system, account or information an attacker attempts to compromise |

We also discussed different categories of cyber attackers, including cybercriminals, hacktivists, state-sponsored attackers, script kiddies and insider threats.

## 5. Types of Hackers

I learned the distinction between three common categories of hackers:

- **White Hat:** Ethical hackers who conduct authorised security assessments.
- **Black Hat:** Malicious hackers who exploit vulnerabilities for illegal or harmful purposes.
- **Grey Hat:** Individuals who may identify vulnerabilities without proper authorisation.

An important takeaway was that **authorisation and ethical conduct are essential in cybersecurity testing**.

## 6. Data Privacy and Personal Security

The session highlighted how valuable personal information is to cybercriminals and other organisations.

Cyber attackers may target passwords, financial information, personal records, sensitive files and system access.

Some security practices discussed include:

- Using strong passwords and two-factor authentication.
- Keeping devices and software updated.
- Avoiding suspicious links.
- Using password managers.
- Taking precautions when using public Wi-Fi.

## 7. Personal Reflection

Today's lesson helped me understand that cybersecurity is much broader than hacking. It involves protecting information, managing access, identifying threats and applying preventive security measures.

The CIA Triad and AAA Framework were particularly interesting because they explain two important aspects of information security: **what needs to be protected and how access should be controlled**.

## Key Takeaway

Cybersecurity begins with understanding the value of information, recognising potential threats and applying appropriate measures to protect digital systems and data.
