# Day 12/30 — Practical Assignment: McKesson Healthcare Data Breach

**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Academy:** TS Academy  
**Module:** Foundations of Cybersecurity and Computing II
**Activity:** Cybersecurity Practical Assignment  
**Topic:** Social Engineering, Vishing and Data Protection

## Overview

Today, I worked on a practical assignment from TS Academy that involved researching the 2026 McKesson healthcare data breach.

The purpose was to understand how attackers can exploit human behaviour to gain unauthorised access to systems and why protecting sensitive information is important.

I reviewed news reports about the incident and answered questions about the attack method, potentially exposed information, its risks and possible preventive measures.

## 1. How Did the Attackers Gain Access?

According to reports, the attackers claimed to have used **vishing (voice phishing)**, a social engineering technique involving deceptive phone calls.

They reportedly manipulated McKesson employees into revealing login credentials, which allowed unauthorised access to company accounts and connected applications.

Although McKesson confirmed unauthorised access and data theft, the reported vishing method came from the attackers' claims.

## 2. What Information Was Exposed?

Reports identified the following categories of potentially compromised information:

- Patients' full names
- Residential addresses
- Phone numbers and email addresses
- Dates of birth
- Social Security numbers
- Patient identification numbers
- Medical records and diagnoses
- Medication and prescription information
- Health insurance details
- Billing and other healthcare-related information

The complete scope of the exposed data was not independently confirmed in the initial incident reports.

## 3. Why Is This Information Sensitive?

I focused on **medical records** because they contain confidential information about an individual's health, diagnoses and treatments.

If exposed, this information could lead to privacy violations, identity theft, medical fraud or misuse of sensitive personal details.

This helped me understand why healthcare organisations must take data confidentiality seriously.

## 4. How Can Individuals Prevent Vishing Attacks?

One important precaution is to avoid sharing passwords, login credentials or verification codes with unexpected callers.

If someone claims to represent a trusted organisation, I should end the call and contact that organisation through its official, independently verified communication channels.

## 5. Connection to Cybersecurity Concepts

This assignment reinforced several concepts covered during my TS Academy classes:

**Social Engineering:** Attackers may manipulate people rather than directly exploiting technical weaknesses.

**Attack Vectors:** Phone calls can serve as a pathway for gaining unauthorised access.

**Confidentiality:** Protecting personal and medical information is an essential part of the CIA Triad.

**Risk Management:** Organisations must identify potential threats, assess their impact and introduce appropriate security measures.

## Key Takeaway

My biggest lesson from this assignment was that cybersecurity is not only about securing computers and networks. Human awareness is equally important.

An organisation may have strong technical security measures, but a successful social engineering attack can still put sensitive information at risk.

This case study helped me connect what I have learned in class to a real-world cybersecurity incident.
