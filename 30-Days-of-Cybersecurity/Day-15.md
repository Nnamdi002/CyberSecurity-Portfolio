Today marks Day 15 of my 30 Days of Learning Challenge with TS Academy.

When I started this journey, I had limited knowledge of cybersecurity. Over the past two weeks, I have explored foundational concepts through TS Academy classes, independent learning on TryHackMe and a real-world cybersecurity case study.

## 1. Key Concepts Learned So Far

### Cybersecurity Fundamentals

I learned that cybersecurity involves protecting systems, networks and information against unauthorised access, attacks and disruption.

I was introduced to the CIA Triad:

- **Confidentiality:** Ensuring information is accessible only to authorised individuals.
- **Integrity:** Protecting information against unauthorised changes.
- **Availability:** Ensuring systems and information remain accessible when needed.

### Social Engineering and Cyber Threats

I explored how attackers manipulate human behaviour through techniques such as phishing, vishing, baiting and impersonation.

One important lesson was that attackers do not always need to exploit technical vulnerabilities. Sometimes, they target people to obtain sensitive information or access.

### Risk Management

I learned about identifying cybersecurity risks, assessing their likelihood and impact, and responding through avoidance, mitigation, transfer or acceptance.

### Operating System Fundamentals

Through TS Academy and TryHackMe, I explored Windows and Linux fundamentals, including file systems, user accounts, permissions and built-in security features.

Understanding these concepts has helped me recognise the importance of access control in protecting computer systems.

## 3. My Biggest Lesson

One of the most important things I have learned is that cybersecurity is not only about technology.

Human behaviour, security awareness and everyday decisions also play important roles in protecting information.

Actions such as sharing login credentials, clicking suspicious links or ignoring security updates can introduce risks.

This has encouraged me to become more conscious of security practices in my everyday use of technology.
