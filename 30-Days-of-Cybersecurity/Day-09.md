## Overview

Today's class focused on cyber threats, attack vectors, social engineering, risk management, cybersecurity career pathways and professional ethics.

## 1. Attack Vectors and Cyber Threats

An **attack vector** is the method or pathway an attacker uses to gain access to a system and exploit its weaknesses.

We explored several threat categories, including:

- Unauthorised access
- Malware, including viruses, worms, Trojans and ransomware
- Denial-of-Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks
- System design failures
- Human error and social engineering

## 2. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into revealing sensitive information or performing actions that compromise security.

Examples covered included phishing, pretexting, baiting, tailgating, shoulder surfing and dumpster diving.

**Key warning signs:** Suspicious links, urgent requests, unfamiliar senders, unrealistic offers and requests for confidential information.

## 3. Risk Management

Risk management involves identifying, assessing and responding to threats that could affect an organisation's systems and information.

We learned four risk treatment strategies:

| Strategy | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Avoidance | Stop the activity creating the risk |
| Mitigation | Reduce the likelihood or impact |
| Transfer | Shift some financial consequences to another party |
| Acceptance | Knowingly retain the risk |
